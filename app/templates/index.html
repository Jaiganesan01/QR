Education & Defense

Think Before You Scan

Attackers use QR codes because they hide the final web address from immediate view. Before trusting an unfamiliar QR code, keep these core habits in mind:

  • Preview the decoded destination: Always check the web address before opening it or submitting any information.
  • Check domain spelling carefully: Attackers often substitute visually similar letters or add extra subdomains to mimic trusted sites.
  • Be cautious with payment and login prompts: Be alert whenever an unexpected QR code demands payment, passwords, or one-time verification codes (OTPs).
  • Avoid entering sensitive information: If a scanned QR opens an unprompted form asking for credentials, close it immediately.

Physical QR Safety Tip

In public places like restaurants, parking meters, and bus stations, check whether the QR code is an adhesive sticker placed over an original sign.

Tampering Check: If a sticker peels off or covers an existing QR graphic, do not scan it.

Responsible Security & Privacy

QShield is designed to assist your inspection of QR payloads before you interact with them. To use the platform responsibly, keep these principles in mind:

• A low risk score does not guarantee safety: A low score simply means that no obvious suspicious heuristic patterns were detected. Zero detection system can guarantee 100% protection.
• HTTPS alone does not prove trustworthiness: Malicious websites frequently use valid SSL certificates. HTTPS encrypts traffic; it does not verify intent.
• Evidence-based heuristic boundaries: Findings reflect available static evidence and rule implementations, not calibrated probabilities.
• Respect personal information: Avoid submitting QR codes containing private keys, personal passwords, or confidential identity documents.
